Reddit saves to a Zettelkasten

I had years of saved Reddit posts and never went back to any of them. So I wrote a local pipeline that pulls them down, turns each into an atomic note, and links the notes by the ideas they share, the way Niklas Luhmann's slip-box worked. Obsidian draws the result as a graph.

The vault, live

333 real notes from the August 2025 export, with the 45 concepts they share most. Drag a node; hover or tap one to read it.

My saves weren't all work-related, so this shows the 333 from tech, study, career, film and iOS subreddits, with titles checked by hand. Only titles, subreddits and keywords are published; no post text, comments or usernames.

How it works

Python end to end, running on my own machine. Nothing goes to a server.

  1. ScrapePRAW signs in to Reddit with OAuth and pulls each saved post: title, body, top comments, media and metadata. 939 of 1,065 came back; 117 were refused (removed or private) and 9 hit server errors.
  2. Find the ideasTF-IDF picks the words that make each post distinctive, with its settings adjusted to the size of the collection. Heuristic tags (technical, problem, emotional, solution) and optional sentence-transformer embeddings add a second layer.
  3. LinkEvery keyword becomes a concept note with wikilinks both ways. Cosine similarity fills each post's "related" list, and keywords that appear together become concept-to-concept edges.
  4. WeighNetworkX scores every node by degree, betweenness, clustering and PageRank, combined into one weight that sets its size in the graph.
  5. GroupCommunity detection folds related concepts into "syndicates", so a thousand-node graph can be read as a handful of themes.
  6. Keep my editsEach note stores a hash of its source. A re-run only rewrites notes whose source changed, so anything I add by hand survives.

What it gets wrong

From the export's own graph files.

  • "Deleted User" names half the syndicates. Each syndicate is labelled with its most common terms, and author names were in the term list. Deleted accounts all share one name, so 9 of the 18 syndicates, including the five biggest, are called "Deleted User".
  • URLs leak into the concepts. https, com, png, webp and preview are among the 25 most common concepts, because links were tokenised like words. Reddit's x200b (an encoded blank line) shows up too.
  • Too many concepts. 3,427 concept notes for 667 posts. Many appear in only a few posts and make the full graph a hairball, which is the problem syndicates were meant to fix.
  • Saves don't wait. 117 posts were already gone when I fetched them. A save is a link, not a copy.
  • What I'd change: strip URLs and author fields before TF-IDF, keep only concepts above a minimum document count, and archive each post's text the day it's saved.
